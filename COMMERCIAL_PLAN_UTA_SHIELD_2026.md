# UTA Shield / MarketNow — commercial validation plan
Updated 17 November 2026. AliceLabs LLC, built by Edison Flores from Quito, Ecuador.

## Buyer and problem
The initial buyer is a CTO, platform lead or SaaS provider publishing an HTTP MCP server. The operator needs request identity, per-tool permissions, controls over sensitive output and a reproducible audit trail.

## Current implementation
The public UTA Shield gateway implements HMAC request authentication, per-tool access controls, DLP checks, rate limiting and JSONL audit logs. ATC signed credentials and the UTA adapter are related components with distinct roles and licenses. JSONL gateway records are not all independently signed receipts. Reputation scoring and other roadmap features are not represented as completed deployment capabilities.

Source, scope and license: https://github.com/eddyflores100-lang/uta-shield

## Entry offer
The published entry offer is US$1,000 setup plus optional US$100/month/server support. The proposed pilot covers one staging HTTP MCP server, with US$500 upfront and US$500 against agreed delivery. Proposed timing is five business days after scope, access and initial payment are confirmed.

Before a paid engagement, the parties agree:
- Server and authentication flow, authorized test environment and access.
- Controls and test cases included in the pilot.
- Acceptance criteria, delivery evidence and service terms.
- Data handling, infrastructure costs, support boundaries and any changes to scope.

Deliverables are reproducible configuration, agreed integration tests, a findings report and a written handover. A pilot is a scoped engineering service; it is not independent security certification or a guarantee that a server cannot be compromised.

## Commercial validation
The business is pre-revenue: no paying customers or recurring revenues are reported. Public software, documentation and a published deployment offer are evidence of the build; they are not customer traction.

The proposed first milestone is three paid pilots. Technical collaboration conversations and proposals are not counted as sales or signed contracts.

For each pilot, record:
- Buyer role and active integration need.
- Response-to-scope and scope-to-payment conversion.
- Actual engineering hours and direct infrastructure/testing costs.
- Passed and failed acceptance tests, customer feedback and rework.
- Demand for optional ongoing support and measured support time.

## Proposed economics to test
For a US$1,000 pilot, a planning assumption is eight hours at US$25 plus US$100 of testing/infrastructure. That is US$300 of direct cost and US$700 of contribution before selling costs, tax, administration and warranty obligations. These are unmeasured assumptions to replace with actual delivery costs. No gross margin from completed customer work is claimed.

The US$100/month support offer is optional. It must have an agreed response scope and usage boundary. It is not current recurring revenue. Model adoption, cancellations and delivery cost separately.

## First 90 days
Days 1–14: qualify an operator, agree scope and seek the first advance payment.

Days 15–30: deliver a pilot, document costs and test results, and obtain acceptance.

Days 31–60: seek the second and third paid pilots while correcting repeated installation issues.

Days 61–90: offer optional support to satisfied buyers, measure demand and expand only the repeatable work.

These are working goals, not a promise of sales or a guaranteed deadline for customer acquisition.
